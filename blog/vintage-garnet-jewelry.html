Garnet is the January birthstone, the traditional gem for a second wedding anniversary, and one of the most wearable red stones in vintage jewelry. The name comes from the Latin granatus, meaning pomegranate seed, a reference to the small, dark red crystals the stone often forms in.

Vintage garnet jewelry spans a wide range. You can find a sterling silver garnet ring for under $20, a heart-shaped garnet in solid 10k gold, Victorian Revival bracelets from the mid-century, and Austro-Hungarian pieces from the Bohemian garnet tradition. Below are fourteen finds, from $19 to $319, with notes on what to look for in each.

Garnet doesn't try to look like a ruby. Its red is darker and warmer, and it has been worn for centuries.

How to tell real garnet from glass

Check the metal first. A 925 or 10K hallmark suggests a piece made to be fine jewelry, and fine jewelry is more likely to hold a genuine stone. Gold-tone costume pieces, such as the Florenza bracelets above, are more likely to use glass or simulated stones and are collected for their design.

Look at the stone. Glass often looks too perfect and uniform, and sometimes has tiny round bubbles under magnification. Natural garnet can have small inclusions. Neither is definitive on its own, so a jeweler with a loupe is the best way to be sure.

Know the old tricks. In antique pieces, some garnets are doublets, a thin slice of garnet on top of a glass base. The seller's description and photos help, and an Austro-Hungarian or Bohemian piece is best bought from a seller who describes the stones in detail.

Read what the seller says. "Real red garnet," "genuine garnet" and "garnet-colored" mean different things. When the description isn't clear, ask. A good vintage seller will answer.

Garnet jewelry questions

What month is garnet the birthstone for?

Garnet is the birthstone for January. It is also the traditional gemstone for a second wedding anniversary, which makes garnet jewelry a natural gift for either occasion.

Is vintage garnet jewelry real garnet?

Often, but not always. Fine jewelry in 10k gold or sterling silver is more likely to hold a genuine garnet, while mid-century costume jewelry may use red glass. If the listing says genuine garnet, look for a hallmark such as 925 or 10K. For certainty, a jeweler with a loupe can check the stone.

Does garnet only come in red?

No. Red is the best-known color, but garnet also occurs in orange, purple, pink and green. The deep red stones in vintage jewelry are the most common type.

How do I care for garnet jewelry?

Wash it gently in warm, soapy water with a soft brush, then dry it with a soft cloth. Avoid harsh chemicals and sudden temperature changes. Garnet is fairly hard, ranging from about 6.5 to 7.5 on the Mohs scale, but it can still be chipped by knocks, so take rings off for heavy work.
